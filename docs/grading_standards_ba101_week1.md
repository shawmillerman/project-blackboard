# BA 101 Week 1 Grading Standards

## Overall Philosophy

For BA 101, Week 1, grading prioritizes **conceptual understanding over vocabulary recitation**. Students are not required to explicitly label each factor of production if the concept is clearly and correctly described in plain language.

This is an **applied business understanding exercise**, not a terminology quiz.

---

## Explicit Terms vs. Implicit Understanding

### Core Rule

**Clear description of a factor = credit earned, even without explicit term usage.**

Students may describe factors of production using plain language or colloquial terms, provided the meaning is unambiguous and textbook-correct.

### Example: Labor Factor

✓ **Acceptable (implicit reference):**
- "workers to design, shape, and sell the boards"
- "people involved in making the product"
- "the team that builds it"

These are unambiguous descriptions of labor. No confusion, no stretching of the concept. Full credit.

---

## Factors Requiring Explicit Naming or Clear Linkage

Certain factors benefit from **explicit call-out** due to common student misunderstandings:

### Entrepreneurship
- **Why explicit naming matters:** Often confused with labor or management
- **Standard:** Strongly prefer explicit identification
- **Accept if:** Student clearly distinguishes entrepreneurial decisions (risk-taking, innovation, resource allocation) from labor or management roles

### Capital
- **Why explicit naming matters:** Frequently misunderstood as "money only"
- **Standard:** Require explicit linkage to tools, equipment, supplies, and/or infrastructure
- **Accept if:** Student clearly differentiates between financial capital and physical/human capital, OR provides concrete examples of equipment/resources used

### Natural Resources
- **Why explicit naming matters:** Easily overlooked or conflated with labor/capital
- **Standard:** Explicit identification strongly preferred
- **Accept if:** Student clearly identifies raw materials or environmental inputs (e.g., "ocean/waves for surfing")

---

## When to Penalize

Apply grade penalties **only when:**

1. **Ambiguity exists:** The meaning is unclear or could be misinterpreted
2. **Incorrect usage:** The student applies a factor incorrectly or demonstrates conceptual confusion
3. **Conceptual gaps:** A required factor is completely missing or clearly misunderstood
4. **Weak reasoning:** Explanation lacks depth or clarity about how the factor applies to the business

---

## Guidance to AI for Calibration

When calibration examples reference this standard, the AI should:

1. ✓ Accept implicit references when the concept is clearly demonstrated
2. ✓ Award full credit for correct, unambiguous descriptions
3. ✗ Penalize only when ambiguity, incorrect usage, or conceptual gaps are present
4. ✓ Maintain flexibility in Week 1 while preserving college-level rigor

## Summary Quick Reference

| Factor | Explicit Naming Required? | Why |
|--------|---------------------------|-----|
| **Labor** | No (if clear) | Rarely confused; plain language usually sufficient |
| **Capital** | Yes (or clear examples) | Often misunderstood as "money only" |
| **Entrepreneurship** | Yes (or clearly distinguished) | Easily confused with labor/management |
| **Natural Resources** | Yes (preferred) | Easily overlooked; explicit helps clarity |
| **Land/Location** | No (if clear) | Usually obvious in context |
